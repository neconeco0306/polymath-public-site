01MISSING HYPOTHESIS LAB

An AI grew confident without noticing its starting assumptions were wrong.

We asked an AI to choose the right hypothesis. The real explanation was missing from its list.

Before / comparison

Sampling only where listed hypotheses disagree raised an alarm in 17 of 300 synthetic runs.

What happened

Reserving 25% of observations for broad coverage raised alarms in 300 of 300 runs under that same setting.

Takeaway

Being good at choosing among options is not the same as checking whether the right option exists.

See method and limitations

How we tested it Four sampling policies, seven synthetic generators, 300 paired random seeds; headline result uses a 24-observation budget.

What remains uncertain The usual policy won in another setting, and the hybrid struggled with only 12 observations. A separate noise-calibration test had a defect. No independent replication.

02AEGL-NOISE-001

An AI can report a performance win even when nothing improved.

We held software performance perfectly constant. Repeating noisy tests still produced apparent improvements.

Before / comparison

With 5% simulated measurement noise, 81.79% of 10,000 initial runs falsely met a 5% improvement goal.

What happened

In a separate follow-up, checking a frozen candidate using nine fresh measurements reduced false confirmations to 0.63%.

Takeaway

Choosing the best result after many tries can turn random noise into a convincing—but false—success story.

See method and limitations

How we tested it Gaussian simulation with true latency fixed at 2.5 seconds: up to 50 candidates, three measurements each, 10,000 runs per noise level. Fresh follow-up measurements.

What remains uncertain Not a measured error rate for real Claude agents or websites. The 0.63% came from a separate predefined follow-up; independent real-world validation is pending.

81.79% → 0.63%Synthetic, two separate studies
03FIELD EXPERIMENT 002

We searched more than 64,000 light measurements for a planet's shadow.

A planet crossing in front of a star can leave a tiny, recurring dip in its recorded brightness.

Before / comparison

We explored periods from 3 to 20 days in 64,814 rows of public, third-party processed Kepler observations—without entering the known answer.

What happened

After inspecting a roughly 18.973-day candidate, we explored its half-period, 9.4874 days, close to a known planet's ~9.49 days.

Takeaway

Things we cannot photograph directly may still leave measurable repeating traces.

See method and limitations

How we tested it Processed Kepler-227 light-curve data; compared temporal halves and an intentionally wrong 10.2-day control period.

What remains uncertain The planet was already known. The half-period step was post-hoc and the later data were consulted during analysis—not a blind discovery. Original NASA archive files remain unmatched.

9.4874 daysKnown planet, re-analysis
04EQS-001

A simple rule beat a more elaborate search algorithm.

We tested sophisticated search, then found that basic swap-and-cancel simplifications sometimes did better.

Before / comparison

Search method D reduced cost by 62.41% on average in the original 16 synthetic circuit tasks.

What happened

A simple reference rule reduced cost by 65.54% on the same tasks.

Takeaway

A new, complicated method does not earn its complexity until it beats a fair simple baseline.

See method and limitations

How we tested it Offline tests on classical reversible circuits; additional 32-task exploration showed that rankings change across task families.

What remains uncertain Not quantum hardware evidence. The additional tasks were designed after initial results, and independent review remains outstanding.

65.54% vs 62.41%Conditional experiment
05EL-001 / EL-002

Ten pieces of evidence may trace back to just one source.

If ten sites copy the same article, they are not ten independent witnesses.

Before / comparison

A structural gate accepted ten synthetic records copied from one declared source.

What happened

A prototype lineage checker traced the declared records to one shared origin.

Takeaway

Count independent origins, not just documents or confident-looking citations.

See method and limitations

How we tested it Synthetic controls plus metadata from 31 public sources; matched 7 of 8 same-origin pairs in a separate 12-source set.

What remains uncertain A simple title-and-first-author baseline also got 7/8. Superiority is unproven, and declared origins are not authenticated.

10 copies ≠ 10 sourcesConditional experiment
06BUDGET ROUTING v1.8

A control experiment overturned our own explanation of an improvement.

We added a minimum-allocation rule to an AI compute budget scheme. But did we actually need it?

Before / comparison

With the extra floor, budget allocations looked feasible and well-behaved.

What happened

Without the floor, all 50 synthetic conditions were feasible too. We retracted the earlier explanation.

Takeaway

To know whether a change helped, remove it and compare against a matched baseline.

See method and limitations

How we tested it Ten budget levels × five alias counts, comparing log/square-root allocation curves with and without minimum floors.

What remains uncertain The floor prevented four zero allocations. Whether that improves real tasks is unknown. This is a finite synthetic diagnostic without independent certification.

50 / 50Conditional experiment

Ask a testable question

03

Do not overclaim
